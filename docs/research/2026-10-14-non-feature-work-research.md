## What the sources suggest

1. **A commit type is not a change record.** Conventional Commits classify intent, while semantic-release and Changesets decide publication/release impact; neither proves completion or behavior preservation. [Conventional Commits](https://www.conventionalcommits.org/en/v1.0.0/) · [semantic-release](https://github.com/semantic-release/semantic-release) · [Changesets](https://github.com/changesets/changesets)
2. **Non-feature work needs a different acceptance statement, not no acceptance statement.** Typical invariants are “external behavior is unchanged,” “the supported runtime/lockfile remains reproducible,” “the migration is reversible,” “the new toolchain passes CI,” or “the architectural decision is accepted.” Kiro’s EARS requirements, Jira’s DoD, and BMAD’s “what must not change” guidance all support writing those conditions explicitly. [Kiro requirements-first](https://kiro.dev/docs/specs/feature-specs/requirements-first/) · [Atlassian DoD](https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/definition-of-done) · [BMAD planning path](https://docs.bmad-method.org/plan/choose-a-planning-path/)
3. **Behavior verification is strongest when it is repeatable and bound to the change.** Spec Kit’s bug workflow requires the original reproduction; Tessl saves scenarios as regression tests; Fowler and Feathers describe a runnable suite and characterization tests as refactoring safety nets. [Spec Kit bugfix](https://github.github.io/spec-kit/guides/bugfix.html) · [Tessl scenarios](https://docs.tessl.io/improving-your-skills/evaluate-skill-quality-using-scenarios) · [Fowler testing](https://martinfowler.com/testing/) · [Feathers](https://michaelfeathers.silvrback.com/characterization-testing)
4. **Approval should attach to judgment.** Good candidates are intent/scope, architectural decisions, release intent, and changed golden baselines. Automated tests should provide evidence, not be duplicated by a ceremonial approval gate. This is consistent with OpenSpec’s reviewed plan, Kiro’s phase confirmations, Shape Up’s betting table, ADR stakeholder acceptance, and BMAD’s explicit plan/acceptance gates. [OpenSpec](https://github.com/Fission-AI/OpenSpec/blob/main/README.md) · [Kiro](https://kiro.dev/docs/specs/feature-specs/requirements-first/) · [Shape Up](https://basecamp.com/shapeup/2.2-chapter-08) · [ADR](https://github.com/joelparkerhenderson/architecture-decision-record) · [BMAD](https://docs.bmad-method.org/build/finish-an-epic/)
5. **Scale ceremony to risk.** OpenSpec allows a truly trivial one-line fix to skip ceremony; BMAD says trivial edits may skip process; Kiro Quick Spec removes phase gates for well-understood work; Shape Up bounds work with appetite rather than exhaustive task up-front. [OpenSpec overview](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Fission-AI/OpenSpec/main/docs/overview.md) · [BMAD build](https://docs.bmad-method.org/build/build-a-change/) · [Kiro Quick Spec](https://kiro.dev/docs/specs/quick-spec/) · [Shape Up shaping](https://basecamp.com/shapeup/1.1-chapter-02)

## Candidate canon models

### Model A — First-class `chore` change kind with explicit no-delta evidence (recommended)

**Shape**

- Add a `chore` (or `maintenance`) change kind. Its required brief is short but explicit: intent, category (`refactor`, `dependency`, `build/tooling`, `CI`, `infra`, docs), in/out scope, and “must not change” invariants.
- A chore may have **zero scenario deltas**, but that fact is explicit (OpenSpec’s `skip_specs: true` is the closest established precedent). It still has tasks and an evidence plan.
- The evidence record binds the commit to checks: existing scenario suite/regression suite, characterization or golden snapshots where needed, dependency/build reproducibility checks, migration/rollback checks, or deployment/health checks for infra.
- A behavior-changing chore must use ordinary scenario deltas (or be reclassified as a feature/fix). A decision-heavy architecture/toolchain choice may additionally link an ADR.
- Keep human approval on the brief for non-trivial chores; require a human decision to update approved baselines or to waive a failed/absent check. Automated evidence remains the ship guard.

**Tradeoffs**

- **Pros:** closes canon’s current unchecked-task gap; preserves the distinction between behavior specs and implementation/operational work; makes no-delta intent auditable; supports the strongest evidence practice without inventing fake scenarios; supports refactors, dependency bumps, build migrations, and infra.
- **Cons:** an existing scenario suite may have weak coverage; infra/tooling evidence needs category-specific adapters; adding a change kind and evidence schema costs implementation complexity; human review of every non-trivial chore remains overhead.

### Model B — No-brief fast path for low-risk chores

**Shape**

- Permit a narrowly defined fast path for formatting, comment/docs-only edits, lockfile refreshes with no supported-version or runtime change, or other low-risk work. The commit/PR records category and the exact deterministic check command(s); the normal CI suite must pass.
- If the change touches public API, runtime support, architecture, deployment, security, or behavior, it is ineligible and must use a chore brief.

**Tradeoffs**

- **Pros:** follows OpenSpec/BMAD proportionality and avoids turning every small maintenance edit into ceremony; useful for high-frequency chores.
- **Cons:** classification is easy to game; no durable intent/scope artifact; “low risk” can hide transitive dependency, build, or infra impact; weak auditability; it can recreate the current canon fall-through if the eligibility rules are vague.

### Model C — ADR-style maintenance record

**Shape**

- Treat every non-feature change as a lightweight decision record: context/problem, decision, alternatives, consequences, scope, acceptance, and evidence. Use full ADR status/immutability only for architecture/toolchain decisions; use a smaller maintenance record for routine chores.
- Ship requires the record to be accepted and its evidence attached. Scenarios are optional only when the record states why no observable contract changes; regression/characterization evidence is required for behavior-preserving refactors.

**Tradeoffs**

- **Pros:** excellent long-term explanation for dependency/toolchain/infra/architecture migrations; explicit stakeholder acceptance and consequences; naturally records why a behavior delta was intentionally absent.
- **Cons:** overkill for routine chores; ADRs are decision artifacts, not test/evidence systems; risks duplicating `brief.md` and `tasks.md`; requires a policy distinguishing decisions from mechanical edits.

## Recommendation

Adopt **Model A**, with two deliberate escape hatches borrowed from Models B and C:

1. **Default:** every non-trivial non-feature change is a `chore` change with a human-approved brief, tasks, and a required evidence plan. The brief MUST state what is intentionally unchanged and what category-specific outcome is required.
2. **No behavior delta:** allow an explicit `no-spec-delta` marker (OpenSpec’s `skip_specs: true` is a proven convention), but never let it mean “no verification.” Ship evidence should include the existing scenario/regression suite and targeted checks appropriate to the category.
3. **Behavior-changing work:** require scenario deltas and route it through the normal feature/fix gate. A refactor that changes an external contract is not a no-delta chore.
4. **Tiny low-risk work:** allow a tightly scoped no-brief path only when the change meets mechanically checkable eligibility rules and records the check result; disallow it for public API, runtime, architecture, deployment, security, or irreversible migrations.
5. **Decision-heavy work:** attach an ADR when the change chooses or supersedes an architectural/toolchain/operational decision. ADR acceptance is additive; it does not replace behavior evidence.
6. **Evidence semantics:** an evidence report should record commit/change identity, commands or checks run, outputs/status, scenarios/regression suites exercised, baseline/snapshot approval if applicable, and residual risk. For an existing suite, “all scenarios passed at the new commit” is useful evidence but should not be claimed as complete coverage; characterization/golden tests are the required supplement where the suite does not express the preserved behavior.

This keeps canon’s central invariant—shipping requires independent evidence—while making non-feature work first-class instead of allowing it to disappear into unchecked task rows.
